TESTIMONY OF DEFENSE ATTORNEY SAMUELS
Cross-Examination - Defense

Q: Attorney Samuels, your perspective on the case?
A: The prosecution's timeline relies heavily on shifting testimony and circumstantial evidence that doesn't conclusively prove intent or action.

Q: What about witness coercion concerns?
A: Some witnesses gave statements only after extended interviews. The distinction between questioning and pressure can blur. We have evidence that at least one witness initially denied seeing the defendants but later changed their story.

Q: Are you disputing witness presence?
A: I'm disputing certainty. Presence doesn't equal action. The prosecution asks jurors to infer participation from mere presence, which isn't sufficient for conviction beyond reasonable doubt.

Q: What's your alternative theory?
A: The explosion may have been accidental. The defendants may have been trying to prevent a disaster, not cause one. The prosecution hasn't ruled out accidental causes in their investigation.

Q: How does this create reasonable doubt?
A: If the prosecution's case relies on inference rather than direct evidence, and if alternative explanations exist, then reasonable doubt has been established.

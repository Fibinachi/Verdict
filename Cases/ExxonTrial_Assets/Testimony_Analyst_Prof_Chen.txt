TESTIMONY OF ANALYST PROF. CHEN
Cross-Examination - Exxon Defense Counsel

Q: You're an independent environmental analyst?
A: Yes, I consult on environmental impact assessments. I wasn't retained by either party - I'm offering objective analysis.

Q: What's your view of the data?
A: The data has significant uncertainties. Correlation doesn't equal causation. Multiple factors contribute to ecosystem changes - climate change, shipping traffic, agricultural runoff.

Q: Can you rule out other sources?
A: No, the available data doesn't conclusively identify the spill as the sole source of contamination. The methodology used by plaintiffs' experts doesn't account for background levels adequately.

Q: What about the timing argument?
A: The "temporal proximity" argument is weaker when you consider that ecosystem responses vary widely in timing. Some impacts appear immediately, others take years to manifest - making causation harder to prove.

Q: Do you doubt the spill occurred?
A: I don't dispute that something happened. I question whether the plaintiffs' experts have proven their specific causation theory with the rigor required for civil litigation.

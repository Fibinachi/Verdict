TESTIMONY OF DEFENSE EXPERT DR. LISA ORTIZ
Cross-Examination - Defense

Q: Dr. Ortiz, your expertise?
A: PhD in Forensic Chemistry, formerly with the State Crime Lab. Now in private practice.

Q: You've reviewed the prosecution's forensic evidence?
A: Yes. While the results are what they are, the interpretation requires caution. DNA mixing, contamination during collection, and statistical assumptions all create room for reasonable doubt.

Q: Isn't it true that forensic methods have known error rates?
A: Every scientific method has limitations. The prosecution's certainty doesn't eliminate these inherent uncertainties. The defense isn't disputing the results; we're questioning the conclusion that only the defendant could produce them.

Q: What alternative explanations do you offer?
A: Secondary transfer - DNA on surfaces touched by multiple people. Contamination during evidence handling. Statistical misinterpretation of mixture profiles. The defense doesn't need to prove alternatives; we need to show reasonable doubt exists.

Q: So you're saying the forensic evidence is unreliable?
A: I'm saying the prosecution's certainty is premature given the known limitations of forensic science.

Juror Behavior

Jurors in Verdict are sophisticated AI-driven agents with individual personalities, biases, and decision-making processes. Understanding how jurors form opinions is key to running a realistic simulation.

Sentiment (0% - 100%)

Sentiment represents the juror's overall attitude toward the case. It is influenced by:

  • Evidence presented and its probative value.
  • Witness testimony and perceived credibility.
  • Attorney arguments and effectiveness.
  • The juror's inherent biases and background.

A higher percentage indicates a more positive sentiment toward the plaintiff/prosecution's case.

72% Sentiment

Verdict Lean (0% - 100%)

Verdict lean indicates which way the juror is leaning:

  • 0% — Strongly favors the Defense.
  • 50% — Neutral / Undecided.
  • 100% — Strongly favors the Plaintiff/Prosecution.

Verdict lean is the primary indicator of how a juror is likely to vote if the trial ended today.

65% Plaintiff Lean

Bias (-1.0 to +1.0)

Bias represents the juror's inherent predisposition:

  • -1.0 — Strong bias toward the Defense.
  • 0.0 — Neutral / No inherent bias.
  • +1.0 — Strong bias toward the Plaintiff/Prosecution.

Bias is influenced by the juror's demographics, background memories, and personality traits. It acts as a baseline that trial events modify over time.

Memories vs. Trial Events

Jurors have two types of memory stores:

  • Background Memories — Pre-existing knowledge, experiences, and beliefs that shape the juror's worldview. These are set when the juror is created.
  • Trial Events — Memories created during the trial process, tagged with Source = "TrialEvent". These include evidence presentations, witness testimony, and arguments.

Both types of memories influence the juror's sentiment and verdict lean, but trial events have a more immediate impact.

Memory Decay (0.95)

Memories in Verdict undergo decay over time. The decay factor is 0.95, meaning that each memory's influence gradually diminishes. This simulates the natural human tendency to forget or deprioritize older information.

More recent memories have a stronger influence on the juror's current sentiment and verdict lean than older ones.

Demographic Influence

A juror's demographics (age, gender, occupation, education, income, region) influence their biases and how they interpret evidence. For example:

  • Education level may affect how a juror processes complex evidence.
  • Occupation may give a juror specialized knowledge relevant to the case.
  • Regional background may influence cultural perspectives and values.

When you use the Generate Jury button, jurors are created with diverse demographics to create a realistic cross-section of the population.

Probative Value Weighting

Verdict distinguishes between raw Evidence Strength (persuasive impact) and Probative Value (legal weight). Each piece of evidence carries both metrics:

  • Evidence Strength — Raw impact scaled by media type. Videos (1.6×) and images (1.4×) have more impact than documents (1.0×).
  • Probative Value — Legal weight accounting for reliability (chain of custody), direct vs. circumstantial, hearsay concerns, and witness credibility. This is the value used when updating juror verdict lean.

Jurors use Probative Value (not raw Evidence Strength) when updating their verdict lean, ensuring that flashy but unreliable evidence doesn't disproportionately sway the jury.

Witness Credibility Assessment

Each juror assesses witness credibility through their own biases and cognitive tendencies — just as real jurors do. The assessment is based on:

  • Education — More educated jurors are more skeptical of eyewitness testimony (JD/PhD jurors discount credibility by up to 8%).
  • Age — Older jurors (55+) are more trusting of authority figures (+8% credibility). Younger jurors (<25) are more skeptical (-5%).
  • Political affiliation — Democratic jurors are slightly more trusting of expert testimony; Republican jurors slightly more skeptical of institutional witnesses.
  • Race & Gender — Implicit in-group bias: jurors tend to find witnesses of their own demographic slightly more credible.
  • Current status — Attentive and focused jurors assess credibility more carefully; bored and distracted jurors are less discriminating.
  • Risk perception — Risk-averse jurors are more skeptical of all testimony.

The juror's perceived credibility is then used to scale the probative value of testimonial evidence — meaning the same witness statement can have different legal weight for different jurors.

Media Type Sensitivity

Different jurors are swayed differently by different types of evidence. Verdict models this with per-juror media sensitivity factors:

  • Age — Younger jurors (<30) respond more to video (+15%) and images (+8%). Older jurors (55+) weigh documents (+10%) and testimony (+8%) more heavily.
  • Education — Highly educated jurors are more skeptical of emotional video appeals (-8%) and prefer documentary evidence (+5%).
  • Media consumption — Heavy TV viewers are more influenced by video evidence (+10%).
  • Attentiveness — Bored jurors discount documents (-10%); focused jurors extract more from both documents (+8%) and testimony (+10%).

These sensitivity factors ensure that a diverse jury panel will react differently to the same piece of evidence, creating realistic deliberation dynamics.

Model Weight Calibration

Verdict uses configurable bias factor weights to calibrate how strongly juror demographics and background influence opinion formation and evidence interpretation. The default values are based on established jury research that identifies which attributes tend to have the largest measurable effects in voir dire and verdict prediction.

  • Age — Older jurors often display greater skepticism and risk aversion, while younger jurors may be more receptive to plaintiff narratives.
  • Gender — Gender can affect empathy, credibility assessments, and perceptions of harm.
  • Education Level — Higher education is strongly correlated with better evidence comprehension and a greater ability to parse complex legal issues.
  • Income Level — Economic status influences attitudes toward damages, liability, and authority.
  • Political Affiliation — Ideology is one of the strongest predictors of juror attitudes about punishment, authority, and personal responsibility.
  • Ethnicity — Race and ethnicity shape credibility judgments, fairness perceptions, and juror empathy.
  • Religion — Religious belief influences moral judgement, harm assessment, and views on wrongdoing.
  • Juror Experience — Prior jury service and familiarity with courtroom process affect confidence, deliberation style, and susceptibility to persuasion.
  • Legal Knowledge — Greater legal familiarity improves evidence interpretation and reduces confusion from instructions.
  • Professional Background — Occupation and expertise affect how jurors weight technical evidence and expert testimony.
  • Community Ties — Local connections and civic engagement influence trust in the process and attachment to community norms.
  • Communication Style — Jurors respond to clarity, narrative coherence, emotional tone, and speaker credibility.

Burden of Proof & Reasonable Doubt

Verdict applies different conviction thresholds based on the case mode:

  • Criminal Cases (Beyond Reasonable Doubt) — The prosecution must prove guilt to a very high standard. A juror must have a Verdict Lean above 85% to vote GUILTY. Jurors between 50% and 85% are in the Reasonable Doubt zone — they may think the defendant is probably guilty, but the prosecution hasn't met its burden, so they vote NOT GUILTY.
  • Civil Cases (Preponderance of Evidence) — The plaintiff must only prove their case is more likely true than not. A juror with a Verdict Lean above 50% votes LIABLE.

This means a juror at 70% lean in a criminal case has reasonable doubt and votes NOT GUILTY, while the same 70% lean in a civil case is a LIABLE vote. The higher criminal threshold reflects real-world legal standards.

Criminal conviction threshold: 85% — only jurors to the right of this line vote guilty.

Civil conviction threshold: 50% — simple majority standard.

Tip: Monitor juror sentiment and verdict lean throughout the trial using the Juror Report. This allows you to adjust your strategy to persuade undecided jurors or reinforce support from favorable ones.
